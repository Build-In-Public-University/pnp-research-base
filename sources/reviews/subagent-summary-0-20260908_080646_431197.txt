## Audit report: *Extending the Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa IPM Blow-Up to Uniformly Space-Time Smooth Forcing*

**Source inspected:** `/Users/leoguinan/Downloads/ipm.pdf` and the extracted text `ipm.txt`. The PDF is 57 pages, dated September 7, 2026. I did not independently reprove or formally verify the argument.

### 1. Exact theorem and claimed result

The central statement is **Theorem 2.1, p. 3**, with the construction completed in **Section 6, pp. 42–48**.

For the forced periodic incompressible porous media equation
\[
\partial_t\rho+u_T(\rho)\cdot\nabla\rho=F
\quad\text{on }[0,1)\times\mathbb T^2,
\]
the paper claims existence of:

- smooth, odd, zero-mean initial data \(\rho_{\rm in}\in C^\infty(\mathbb T^2)\);
- a smooth, odd, zero-mean force
  \[
  F\in C^\infty([0,1]\times\mathbb T^2);
  \]
- a solution satisfying
  \[
  \rho\in C^\infty([0,T]\times\mathbb T^2)
  \quad\text{for every }T<1;
  \]
- a limiting density \(\rho_*\in C^\eta(\mathbb T^2)\) for every \(0\leq\eta<1\), with
  \[
  \rho(t)\to\rho_*\quad\text{in }C^\eta\text{ as }t\uparrow1;
  \]
- but nevertheless
  \[
  \|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{L^\infty}\to\infty,
  \qquad
  \|D_xu_T(\rho(t))\|_{L^\infty}\to\infty.
  \]

Thus the claimed singularity is loss of Lipschitz/\(C^1\) control at finite time, while the density remains bounded and converges in every sub-Lipschitz Hölder space.

### 2. What is genuinely new relative to cited prior work

The closest prior result is Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa, cited as [1], especially their theorem and Remarks 1–2. The present paper explicitly attributes the core mechanism to that work (**pp. 1–3, especially pp. 3–4**):

- multiscale parent/child induction;
- transported phase;
- high-frequency oscillatory packets;
- finite velocity expansions;
- phase-harmonic cancellation;
- frequency-dependent packet amplitudes;
- derivative-sensitive induction.

Relative to [1], the paper claims these concrete extensions:

1. **Periodic domain:** the construction is carried out on \(\mathbb T^2\), rather than \(\mathbb R^2\).
2. **Jointly smooth forcing:** [1] is described as having a force in \(L^\infty_t C^\infty_x\), with higher time regularity only anticipated in Remark 1. This paper claims \(F\in C^\infty([0,1]\times\mathbb T^2)\), including at \(t=1\).
3. **Nested Fourier cutoffs:** the cutoff parent \(P_{q+1}=S_{\mu_{q+1}}\rho^{(q)}\) is used to avoid derivative loss.
4. **Exact cutoff commutator:** the cutoff error is not discarded; it is placed explicitly into the force.
5. **Exact finite-stage identities and series:** the authors claim the solution is built by an explicit series and that no separate nonlinear compactness or nonconstructive limit is required. See equations (5), (154)–(157), (175), and Proposition 6.4.
6. **Mixed time-space induction:** Sections 3–5 and Appendix A develop a two-pass induction intended to prove all mixed derivatives of the force.

The paper also emphasizes a methodological analogy with mollification in convex integration (**Section 2.2, pp. 8–9; reference [39]**), but explicitly says this is only an analogy and does not make a convex-integration or weak-nonuniqueness claim.

The strongest defensible novelty claim is therefore: **an alleged extension of the known forced-IPM blow-up mechanism to the torus with a force smooth jointly in space and time, together with a detailed cutoff-based exact-stage construction.** The paper does not claim to originate the underlying blow-up mechanism.

### 3. Role and regularity of the forcing

The force is central, not incidental.

At each finite stage the authors define the force so that the constructed density solves the equation **exactly**. The force increment consists of:

- the exact child residual;
- the exact Fourier-cutoff commutator;
- remaining multiplier remainders and non-corrected harmonics.

See equations (5), (139)–(140), and (154)–(157), especially **pp. 4, 32–39**.

The force is therefore **engineered from the solution construction**. This is an existential result for a specially designed external force, not blow-up under arbitrary, generic, or physically prescribed smooth forcing. The forcing is chosen to support the cascade while remaining small in successively higher derivative norms.

The claimed regularity is unusually strong:

- all spatial derivatives;
- all time derivatives;
- all mixed space-time derivatives;
- uniform control through the endpoint \(t=1\).

The mechanism is the decay estimate
\[
\|\partial_t^m G_{q+1}(t)\|_{C^k}
\lesssim \lambda_{q+1}^{-1/4}
\]
for an increasing finite range of \(m+k\) at each scale; the exponent \(\beta_q\) decreases to zero so that every fixed derivative order is eventually covered. See (7), Proposition 5.13, equations (164), (176), and Proposition 6.4 (**pp. 5, 39–46**).

Important qualification: the **force is smooth at \(t=1\)**, but the solution is only asserted to be classical on every interval \([0,T]\) with \(T<1\). The endpoint density is only asserted to lie in all \(C^\eta\), \(\eta<1\), not \(C^1\).

### 4. What this does—and does not—say about Navier–Stokes

It does **not** solve, address directly, or materially reduce the Clay Millennium Navier–Stokes problem.

The paper studies **forced inviscid IPM**, a two-dimensional active-scalar model with a Darcy velocity given by a zero-order Fourier multiplier. It is not the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equation, and the theorem is not an unforced result.

Navier–Stokes appears only in contextual remarks and citations. The introduction notes related blow-up mechanisms for **forced hypodissipative Navier–Stokes** and generalized SQG (**pp. 1–2; references [7]–[8]), but those are separate equations and separate works. No implication from Theorem 2.1 to Navier–Stokes regularity or blow-up is established.

In particular, the result does not provide:

- finite-time blow-up for unforced Navier–Stokes;
- finite-time blow-up for standard three-dimensional Navier–Stokes;
- a counterexample to global regularity for the Clay formulation;
- a transfer theorem from IPM to Navier–Stokes;
- evidence for or against the Millennium Problem beyond analogy between certain multiscale mechanisms.

Any news story presenting this as a Navier–Stokes solution would be mathematically misleading.

The paper is unusually explicit about AI assistance (**Introduction, pp. 2–3**):

- Claude was used to identify and reproduce elements of Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa’s proof.
- Claude and Codex helped write the main body.
- The models handled bookkeeping involving inductive orders and constants.
- The authors state that the final work was produced with their ideas and “under the strict direction” of the authors.
- The acknowledgments thank Anthropic (**p. 49**).

The paper also mentions Lean, but only in connection with two **announced forthcoming works** on forced two-dimensional Boussinesq and forced three-dimensional Euler (**Section 1.1, p. 3**). It says those two proofs were “formally verified in Lean,” but gives literal placeholders:
> `[BOUSSINESQ SHA-256]` and `[EULER SHA-256]`

No Lean source, repository, commit, hash, build log, or formal artifact for the present IPM paper is supplied in the inspected PDF. Accordingly:

- there is clear evidence of LLM involvement in drafting and proof-development assistance;
- there is **no documentary evidence in this paper that the IPM theorem itself has been Lean-verified**;
- the forthcoming Boussinesq/Euler Lean claim remains unverifiable from this document because the artifact identifiers are placeholders.

### 6. Key caveats and falsifier-first assessment

- **No independent proof verification:** The report reflects the paper’s stated definitions, construction, and internal proof narrative; I did not check every estimate or reproduce the full induction.
- **Force-dependent theorem:** Because \(F\) is defined from the residuals, the result is not a robustness theorem for externally specified forcing.
- **Heavy threshold architecture:** Many steps rely on unspecified “finite thresholds” such as \(\Lambda_{\rm sep}\), \(\Lambda_{\rm geom}\), \(\Lambda_{\rm flow}\), \(\Lambda_{\rm res}\), and \(\lambda_{\rm th,T}\). The paper argues these are finite and compatible, but the construction is not numerically instantiated.
- **Endpoint distinction:** Smooth forcing through \(t=1\) does not mean the density remains smooth through \(t=1\). The claimed singularity is precisely the failure of \(C^1\) regularity.
- **Model distinction:** Forced IPM is not Navier–Stokes, Euler, Boussinesq, or the Clay equation.
- **Novelty should be stated narrowly:** The core packet-amplification idea is credited to prior work. The paper’s claimed contribution is the periodic and jointly space-time-smooth-force implementation, not a new general blow-up principle.
- **Formal-verification claims need artifacts:** The explicit AI/Lean disclosure is valuable, but any claim of formal verification should await publicly inspectable Lean sources and reproducible builds.

**Bottom line:** Read on its own terms, this is a claimed rigorous construction of finite-time gradient blow-up for a specially forced IPM equation on the torus, with a force smooth in all space-time derivatives. Its potentially significant contribution is the smooth-in-time periodic forcing extension of the Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa mechanism. It is not a Navier–Stokes result, and the paper provides no Lean verification artifact for the IPM theorem itself.
