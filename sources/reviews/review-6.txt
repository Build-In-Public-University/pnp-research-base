## Claimed theorem/result

The paper’s Theorem 1.1 (Section 1.1, PDF pp. 1–2) asserts:

- For every prescribed ring radius \(r_0>0\) and height \(z_0\), there exist \(T_*>0\), a smaller solid torus \(T_R\), smooth compactly supported divergence-free axisymmetric initial data \(u_0\), and an axisymmetric force
  \[
  f\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times[0,T_*])
  \]
  whose spatial support remains inside that one fixed torus.
- The data have nonzero swirl and zero meridional velocity.
- The forced incompressible Euler system
  \[
  \partial_tu+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=f,\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0
  \]
  has a preterminal smooth solution on \([0,T_*)\).
- For every \(\tau<T_*\), \(u\) has bounded \(L^2\) energy and bounded spatial gradient.
- Nevertheless,
  \[
  \|\nabla\Gamma(t)\|_\infty\to\infty,\qquad
  \|\omega(t)\|_\infty\to\infty,
  \]
  and separately
  \[
  \int_0^{T_*}\|\omega(t)\|_\infty\,dt=\infty.
  \]
  Here \(\Gamma=ru_\varphi\) is the circulation and \(\omega=\nabla\times u\).

The paper also claims uniqueness before blow-up among divergence-free, locally space-time Lipschitz solutions with the same data and force, bounded \(L^2\) norm and bounded spatial gradient; competitors need not be axisymmetric and pressure need only be distributional (Theorem 1.1; Proposition 14.3, pp. 108–112).

## Construction mechanism

The construction is a deliberately engineered, forced Euler solution:

1. Rewrite axisymmetric Euler with swirl in volume coordinates \(y=(z,r^2/2)\), using circulation \(\Gamma\), streamfunction \(\Psi\), reduced vorticity \(\xi\), and a variable elliptic operator (Section 2, pp. 3–7).
2. Start from a compactly supported rotating base field.
3. Add successive localized oscillatory “layers” at increasing frequencies \(N_m\) and decreasing spatial scales.
4. Transport each new oscillation by the exact nonlinear flow generated by all preceding fields, rather than by a fixed affine background (Sections 3–5, pp. 7–23).
5. Couple a circulation amplitude and an azimuthal-vorticity amplitude through a \(2\times2\) linear amplitude system. The circulation gradient is amplified through the interaction between circulation, its square, and the meridional flow.
6. Use phase inversion and finite correction equations to cancel nonzero oscillatory residuals. The remaining residuals are converted into the physical force through a fixed compactly supported curl inverse (Sections 5–6, pp. 15–27).
7. After each growth phase, choose a return time at which the principal azimuthal-vorticity amplitude and a covector component vanish. This resets the principal vorticity component while preserving the amplified circulation gradient and prepares the next layer (Propositions 4.2 and 7.6–7.7, pp. 14–37).
8. Choose one infinite causal sequence of layers whose insertion times converge to finite \(T_*\). The force increments satisfy summable bounds such as
   \[
   \|f_m\|_{C^k_{x,t}}\lesssim N_m^{-1/2}
   \]
   for sufficiently large layer order, so the force extends smoothly through \(T_*\) (Proposition 12.3, pp. 104–105; Lemma 13.2, pp. 107–108).
9. Lower bounds on the complete circulation gradient over every late time window then imply both norm blow-up and divergence of the vorticity time integral (Section 14, pp. 108–111).

## Inherited versus new

**Inherited or adapted:**

- The broad successive-layer/multiscale singularity program is explicitly attributed to Córdoba and Martínez-Zoroa and related work [5–9] (Section 1.3, pp. 2–3).
- The local two-field amplification idea and smooth-forcing architecture are attributed to the companion Boussinesq construction of Alpọge and Buckmaster [1].
- The phase/Duhamel correction pattern is described as following [1, equation (5.8)] (p. 18).
- The compact divergence/curl recovery operator is based on the operator from [1, Lemma 5.2] (pp. 3–4).
- The paper uses standard Euler identities, material-flow estimates, invariant-cone arguments, Grönwall, and compactness/continuation methods.

**Claimed new contribution:**

- Carrying the layered mechanism out for **3-D axisymmetric Euler with swirl**, whose radial coefficients and elliptic symbol vary spatially.
- Making amplitudes depend on material position and transporting them with the complete corrected nonlinear flow.
- Keeping all correction terms, variable-symbol effects, quadratic-circulation terms, cutoff terms, and full-flow displacement errors in the residual estimates.
- Obtaining a force that is smooth in **all mixed space-time derivatives through \(T_*\)** and supported in one fixed compact torus.
- Starting from everywhere-smooth compactly supported data with nonzero swirl and zero meridional velocity.
- Proving the separate divergent vorticity integral, not merely the two norm limits.
- Proving uniqueness in a non-axisymmetric three-dimensional preterminal class.

The novelty claims should be understood as claims of the manuscript; I did not independently verify the full proof or establish priority against the cited literature.

## Forcing regularity and support

The forcing is not a small perturbation or an incidental error term. It is central to the construction and is selected to sustain each layer.

The paper claims:

- \(f\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^3\times[0,T_*])\), including the terminal time.
- Every mixed space-time derivative is controlled.
- The force has one fixed compact spatial support \(T_R\), uniformly for all \(t\in[0,T_*]\).
- The force is axisymmetric.
- The state variables and vorticity remain supported in the same torus before blow-up.

Relevant references: Theorem 1.1, pp. 1–2; Lemma 2.1, pp. 3–4; Proposition 12.3, pp. 104–105; Lemma 13.2 and Proposition 13.3, pp. 107–108.

A significant technical distinction is that the smooth extension through \(T_*\) is asserted for the **force**, not for the velocity or pressure. The paper explicitly avoids asserting a terminal velocity or pressure state (p. 105).

## Uniqueness scope

The uniqueness result is substantial but narrowly scoped:

- It is preterminal: uniqueness is proved on every \([0,\tau]\) with \(\tau<T_*\), then extended over the preterminal interval.
- Competitors must be divergence-free, locally space-time Lipschitz, solve the forced equation almost everywhere, and satisfy the same \(L^2\) and spatial-gradient bounds.
- Competitors need not be axisymmetric.
- Pressure need not be integrable; the proof avoids pairing the pressure directly with the difference field.
- It does **not** claim uniqueness among arbitrary weak Euler solutions, solutions without the stated bounds, post-blow-up continuations, or solutions for a different force.

See Proposition 14.3, pp. 110–112.

## Relation to unforced Euler and Navier–Stokes

This is **not** the Clay three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes problem.

- The displayed equation is forced Euler, not Navier–Stokes: there is no viscosity term and \(f\) is deliberately constructed and generally nonzero.
- The theorem therefore does not produce a singularity for unforced Euler \(f=0\), nor does it establish anything about smooth global regularity for unforced Navier–Stokes.
- Section 1.3 explicitly distinguishes prior unforced Euler results, which generally have weaker \(C^{1,\alpha}\)-type regularity or different geometries.
- The cited Navier–Stokes result [8] concerns **hypodissipative** Navier–Stokes with small fractional dissipation and a rougher force class; it is not the standard Clay equation (p. 3 and References, p. 112).
- The construction may be conceptually relevant to blow-up mechanisms, but no bridge back to the unforced Euler or standard Navier–Stokes equations is supplied.

## Key caveats

1. **The force is engineered.** The blow-up is for an existentially selected smooth forcing, not for \(f=0\).
2. **No independent proof verification was performed here.** I audited the stated theorem, dependencies, mechanism, and scope from the manuscript; I did not reproduce all 112 pages of estimates or verify every inequality.
3. **The argument is heavily conditional at intermediate stages.** Many propositions assume supplied older-field bounds, support margins, positivity windows, derivative budgets, and scalar comparison conditions. The final claim is that one recursively selected sequence satisfies them all; that global bookkeeping is the proof’s central burden.
4. **The selector is causal but not differentiated.** The least return-time selection is used to construct one history, while the paper explicitly does not claim differentiability of the selector (Proposition 11.1, p. 91; Proposition 12.3, pp. 104–105).
5. **No terminal singular solution is constructed.** The solution is smooth only for \(t<T_*\); the force extends smoothly to \(T_*\).
6. **The uniqueness statement is not a general weak-solution uniqueness theorem.**
7. **The theorem is existential.** It does not provide a natural or canonical forcing, nor show that generic smooth forcing produces blow-up.
8. **The first page has no visible author line or publication status.** The PDF title is clearly *Blowup for the Euler Equations with Smooth Forcing*, but provenance and peer-review status cannot be inferred from the artifact alone.
